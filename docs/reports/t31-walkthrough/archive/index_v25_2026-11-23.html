C · Power on the underside (67 × 60 mm)

Everything on the five power sheets goes underneath (charger, boost, the six bucks, their inductors, protection, hold-off), except the connectors, the USB ESD chip and the charge LED. The top side then holds only the radios, the RV1106 and the connectors, and is only about half full.

What C gains and costs

  • Gains: the smallest board (−10 % against A), the shortest USB and GPS runs, and the charger right behind the DC input and USB-C.
  • Costs, noise: the switching supplies end up close to the radios again (4 mm from the HaLow, 13 mm from the GPS), and the HaLow’s own 3.3 V buck (U11, L11) sits directly under the ESP32-C5 module. Only the inner ground plane separates them from the RF and the RV1106; that works if every switch node stays small and the ground plane under it is unbroken, but it is the opposite of B.
  • Costs, assembly: a heavy second side (QFN charger, boost, inductors, SMA diode, FETs). JLC can do it, but heavy parts on the second side cost more and need care in reflow; it also breaks your Q-4 rule that ICs and inductors stay on top.
  • Costs, rework and probing: the power section, the part most likely to need measuring and fixing on Rev 1, is underneath.
  • This trial is not tuned for SDIO: the RV1106 sits under the ESP32-C5, so SDIO is 28 mm. Moving it under the HaLow/ESP32 boundary would bring it near 20 mm, but the microSD and RV1106 then need about 5 mm more height.

My read

  • Both fix the SDIO problem: about 20 mm instead of 53, and neither the SDIO nor the SPI bus crosses the power area.
  • B (long) is the better electrical layout: the switching supplies are three to six times further from the radios (23 mm from the HaLow, 46 mm from the GPS, against 8 and 15), all antenna pigtails leave from one end, and the USB pair to the connector is the shortest of the three. It costs 5 % more board area and a long 94 × 50 mm shape.
  • A (square) is a compact square board, but its power block sits right beside the GPS and the HaLow.
  • C (power underneath) is the smallest board, but it brings the switching supplies back under and beside the radios and makes the second side heavy. I would only pick it if board size wins over radio noise and easy bring-up.
  • Not settled by either: the SPI to the ESP32-C5 is still 30–40 mm, because the module’s SPI pins run along its whole left edge; the real placement can rotate or shift the module to shorten it.

What changed from the drawn floorplan, and why

  • HaLow U.FL (J901) moved to the left edge, below the top-left hole. The module’s RF pin is on its west edge, so this keeps the RF path short; the four-part low-pass filter sits between them.
  • GPS U.FL (J20) sits under the GPS module, next to its RF pin on the east side. The GPS group is one area now: module, U.FL, the bias inductor L20, both LDOs and their caps.
  • USB-C moved right, next to the microSD socket (your call after asking why it sat in the power area). The edge order is now DC input, PWR, USB-C, microSD. The USB pair from the RV1106 to the connector shrinks from about 40 mm to about 22 mm (straight line) and no longer has to cross the power area or pass the charger inductor. The ESD chip U1 now sits at the connector (3.7 mm from D+), as USB protection should. The charger sits between the two power inputs, so VBUS (up to 3 A) and the DC input both reach it on short, wide copper.
  • The debug header is now three test pads on the bottom (your call): J501 keeps its schematic symbol and nets (RV1106 UART2_M1 TX on pin 79, RX on pin 80, GND), with a new footprint: three bare 1.5 mm pads at 2.54 mm pitch, labelled TX, RX and GND. They take a 2.54 mm pogo clip or soldered wires. No part to buy: excluded from the BOM and the position file. The tall pin header is gone.
  • USB-C now reports its current to the RV1106 (your call): R23 and R24 (100 k) tap the two CC lines into a node, R25 (100 k) to ground divides it by three, and the node runs to the RV1106’s last free ADC input, SARADC_IN1 on pin 22, with a 100 nF filter cap (C508) at the pin. Firmware reads it and raises the charger’s input limit for Type-C sources that advertise 1.5 A or 3 A only on CC (new F-10, H-15). Four 0402 parts on the bottom; C508 sits beside the RV1106, R23–R25 between it and the USB-C.
  • The DC input is now a 2-pin pluggable terminal block (your call; the barrel jack was 185 mm² and the tallest part). Header Kefa KF2EDGR-3.81-2P (C441182, 3.81 mm, right angle, 300 V 8 A) with its mouth flush with the bottom edge; the KF2EDGK-3.81-2P screw plug (C440858, 300 V 8 A, AWG 28–16) ships loose with the board. About 87 mm² of courtyard, 7 mm tall. Users screw in bare wires, or use a pigtail for panels with a 5.5 × 2.1 plug; reverse wiring is caught by the input FET Q11. With an 8 A connector the firmware input-current cap rises from 2.4 A to the charger’s 3.3 A (F-7; the input FETs are rated 3.5 A at 70 °C). The plug sticks out about 9 mm past the board edge when fitted.
  • All four mounting holes are in the corners (your call): MH3 moved to the bottom-left corner, and the DC input and PWR button shifted right to make room. The bottom edge now reads: hole, DC input, PWR, USB-C, microSD, hole. The left edge above the holes holds the HaLow U.FL and the battery and NTC headers.
  • J3 and J4 are now small side-entry JST headers (your choice): J3 battery = 2-pin JST XH, 2.5 mm pitch, 3 A (S2B-XH-A, LCSC C157931); J4 NTC = 2-pin JST PH, 2.0 mm pitch (S2B-PH-K-S, C173752). Right-angle, with the mouth flush with the left edge, so the leads plug in from the side and leave the board sideways. Different pitches, so the two plugs can’t be swapped. They reach about 12 mm (XH) and 8 mm (PH) into the board. The two 11 × 11 mm screw terminals are gone, and the battery-protection and hold-off parts now fit inside their own block. Firmware keeps the charge current at 2.5 A or less (new requirement F-9); the pack lead must be AWG 22.
  • The RV1106 block moved as one piece: chip, bottom decoupling, crystals and keepouts exactly as approved in T29.
  • Stayed on top on purpose: the converters’ input, output and bootstrap capacitors (TI layouts put them next to the IC on the same side), the GPS LDO caps, the RF filter parts and the test points. Everything else small went to the bottom.
  • Four parts didn’t fit inside their own block and went to the nearest free space: the test points TP7, TP17 and TP18, and Q401 (SD power switch). I’ll tidy them when each block is placed by hand.

What this trial is, and what it is not

  • A script packed each block around its main chip, at the spot closest to what each part connects to (hardware/tools/t32_place.py). It proves that the parts fit and shows the shape of the board. It isn’t the final placement: hot loops, sense lines and part orientation get checked block by block against the TI, Rockchip, Espressif and Heltec layouts in the next step.
  • Routing isn’t proven yet. Free top area is about 31 %, and the bottom is mostly free for routing and ground.
  • There is spare room between the ESP32-C5 and the RV1106 and right of the RV1106. Once the blocks are tidy, the board may shrink further, to about 66 × 62 mm.
