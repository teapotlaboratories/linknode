Radios: every supply pin decoupled at the pin, GPS power and antenna at the module

The floorplan already put the three modules where you wanted them, and most of their small parts (the SDIO pull-ups, the ESP32-C5 EN delay) sit under their pins. The radio check found 11 of 27 checks failing. Several decoupling caps had been parked in the power column, up to 70 mm from the pin they serve. The GPS supply was also laid out backwards: its 3.3 V LDO sat 10 mm from the module, while the backup LDO took the spot by the VCC pin. Only the parts that failed moved, each to the best free spot at its pin. 1 check still fails (L20, see open points).

TL;DR

Audio: what is on the board and what the user’s board adds (added 2026-11-23)

The RV1106G has a built-in codec with one line output and two differential mic inputs (HDG §2.3.5.1). It shares pins with the I2S expansion, so only one of the two can be used (HDG §2.3.5 note); LinkNode uses the codec. As Rockchip’s Table 2-12 requires, LinkNode carries a 1 µF capacitor in series on each mic line (C1001–C1004, 0402 X5R, the BOM’s existing 1 µF). The VCM capacitor (C637, 4.7 µF) and the codec’s 1.8 V supply were already fitted. LINEOUT and MICBIAS go straight to their pads, as the table says. All six audio pads have ESD protection (new U1006 on the mic lines; MICBIAS and LINEOUT use spare channels of U1005).

  • Mic: a differential mic or electret connects to MIC0± or MIC1±. An electret also needs bias from MICBIAS through a resistor on the user’s board (typically a few kΩ; check the mic’s datasheet).
  • Line out: line level, for an amplifier on the user’s board; it cannot drive a speaker directly.
  • Pad 31, between the two mic pairs, is GND, as a return for the audio.

Power pads (unswitched, your choice)

3V3 connects to VCC_3V3 (the SoC’s TLV62569, 2 A) and 5V to VCC5V0_SYS (the boost). Neither has a switch or limit, so the user’s board must bring its own protection, and its peripherals are powered whenever the node is. The power budget (docs/power-budget.md) leaves about 0.7 A on the 5 V bus at a nearly empty cell with the radios transmitting. The docs will recommend ≤ 0.5 A total (5 V equivalent) for attached boards, and a fuse or current-limited switch on the user’s board. On battery, the hold-off circuit still turns the whole node off at about 3.3 V, so pad power can’t run the cell flat.
