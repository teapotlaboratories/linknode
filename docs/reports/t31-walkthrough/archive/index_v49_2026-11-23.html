Power flow (the arrows in the layout images point from each section’s power source to its input):
  1. ① DC in (J2, 3.6–24 V) and USB VBUS (J1, 5 V) enter.
  2. ② DC passes the reverse-protection FET Q11 and becomes VDCIN.
  3. ③ VDCIN through Q3/Q4, and USB through Q1/Q2, reach the charger U2’s VBUS pins.
  4. ④ The charger makes VSYS, which feeds the hold-off FET Q9.
  5. ⑤ Q9 switches it to VSYS_SW, the boost U4’s input (via L2).
  6. ⑥ The boost makes VCC5V0_SYS (5 V), carried on the In2 plane to every buck’s input cap and the HaLow FEM switch Q7.
  7. BAT The cells on J3 (VCELL) pass the ship FET Q13 to the charger’s BAT pins (VBAT).

Radios: every supply pin decoupled at the pin, GPS power and antenna at the module

The floorplan already put the three modules where you wanted them, and most of their small parts (the SDIO pull-ups, the ESP32-C5 EN delay) sit under their pins. The radio check found 11 of 27 checks failing. Several decoupling caps had been parked in the power column, up to 70 mm from the pin they serve. The GPS supply was also laid out backwards: its 3.3 V LDO sat 10 mm from the module, while the backup LDO took the spot by the VCC pin. Only the parts that failed moved, each to the best free spot at its pin. 1 check still fails (L20, see open points).

TL;DR

You asked to break out Ethernet, I2C and some GPIO so users can attach their own peripherals, and chose: raw Ethernet pins on the header (no transformer on the board), a 2.54 mm pin header, a dedicated I2C bus, 3.3 V GPIOs, SPI0, power pins with a current limit, ESD protection on every exposed pin, and a second I2C bus on Qwiic. TL;DR
  • What users get: one 2×12 pin header with Ethernet (raw, no magnetics), I2C, SPI, 8 GPIOs and switched 3.3 V / 5 V, plus a Qwiic port on a second I2C bus. Every exposed pin is ESD-protected.
  • What it costs: the board grows 9.5 mm on the right (78 → 87.5 mm wide), about $0.85 in parts plus 5 new LCSC part types, and three on/off signals move to other RV1106 pins.
  • Watch out: users must add an RJ45 with magnetics for Ethernet. 6 of the GPIOs toggle briefly at boot. Both header supplies at full load can push a nearly empty battery past the 5 V bus’s design point, so header power is off until Linux turns it on.
  • You decide: (1) the 9.5 mm growth, (2) the pinout, (3) 0.5 A or 0.4 A per header supply.

Ethernet without magnetics: what is on the board and what the user adds

On the board, as Rockchip’s FEPHY table requires (HDG Table 2-11): a 0 Ω resistor in series on each of the four lines, close to the chip. The guide says these “cannot be omitted”. At the header, a bidirectional TVS across each pair (UDD32C03L01, 1 pF, the same part as Rockchip’s reference). The pairs are routed as 100 Ω differential with less than 30 mil skew over a ground reference (HDG Table 3-2). The 6.04 kΩ EXTR resistor and the PHY supply bead are already fitted.

The user’s adapter (or cable board) must add what Fig 2-22 shows after the TVS: an RJ45 with magnetics (Rockchip uses HR911105A), the two 10 nF center-tap capacitors and the 1000 pF / 3 kV chassis capacitor. Without magnetics the link is not isolated and will not meet the Ethernet spec, so the header label and docs will say so. There are no link LEDs: the RV1106G3 QFN has no ETH_LED pins in Rockchip’s pinout table.

Power budget check

The 5 V bus was sized for 1.8 A at a 3.0 V cell, with a worst case of 1.07 A today (docs/power-budget.md). Both header outputs at their limit add about 0.5 A (5 V) + 0.37 A (3.3 V through its buck), or 0.65 + 0.48 A at the top of the SY6280 tolerance. Together with radio TX peaks on a nearly empty cell, that is above the 1.8 A design point. The boost’s own switch limit (≥ 4.92 A) still holds, so this is a margin and heat question, not a failure. The plan: header power is off by default and switched by EXP_PWR_EN, so firmware can turn it off at low battery and during duty-cycle sleep (F-6). If you prefer, the limits can drop to 0.4 A (Rset 17k), the part’s minimum.
