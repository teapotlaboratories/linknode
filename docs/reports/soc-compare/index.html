RV1106G3 or RK3566?

Should Klepon switch from the RV1106G3 to a chip-down RK3566 with 512 MB or 1 GB of external DRAM, given the routing references available for it? All prices are live from LCSC, 2026-11-20.

Recommendation

Stay with the RV1106. The RK3566 costs more at every memory size and makes the board harder.

  • The RK3566 chip is $10.7 cheaper, but it needs external DRAM, a PMIC and a CPU regulator. Counting those, it costs $3 to $22 more per board than the RV1106G3 (qty 30), before any extra PCB layers.
  • The cheapest RK3566 option relies on a 16-bit memory mode with no proven RK3566 design. Rockchip's guide allows a 16-bit bus only "with the template provided by RK". Every RK3566 board found uses a 32-bit LPDDR4 chip.
  • The routing advantage is small for us. The RV1106 has its DRAM inside the package, so it needs no DDR routing at all. That is the hard part the RK3566 references help with. None of those references is in KiCad, and none uses a 16-bit bus.
  • What the RK3566 really offers: mainline Linux support, 3 SD/SDIO controllers, 4 cores, 1 GB+ of RAM, and a documented 14 mW suspend mode. Those are reasons to switch if they matter more than cost.
  • A cheaper option on the RV1106 side: the RV1106G2 (128 MB) is $9.25 cheaper than the G3 at qty 30. 128 MB is the usual size for an OpenWrt-class router running batman-adv, but we haven't checked it against our software stack.

PCB

JLCPCB quote for an 80 × 60 mm board, 5 pcs: 4-layer $8.00, 6-layer $32.00 (before shipping; jlcpcb.com/quote, 2026-11-20). Rockchip recommends 6 layers for RK3566 + DRAM. A 4-layer board is possible, but only by copying Rockchip's template rules: 3.5 mil traces, 0.2/0.4 mm vias, and ground-guarded DDR signals routed through the power plane. The RK3566's outer ball rows are 0.4 mm pitch. I could not get a volume quote, so the per-board cost of 6 layers at qty 30+ is not in the totals above.

Why the memory choice decides the cost

512 MB

  • The cheapest RK3566 route is one 16-bit DDR3L chip ($10.42). Rockchip's design guides mention 16-bit DDR3/DDR3L/DDR4 only with "the template provided by RK", and that template wasn't found for the RK3566. Rockchip's DDR init tool shows no bus-width option, and no shipping RK3566 board uses it.
  • The safe 32-bit way to get 512 MB is two 2 Gb chips. LCSC has only 9 of the one found in stock.
  • 512 MB LPDDR4 parts (K4F4E3S4HF) are out of stock at LCSC.

1 GB

  • The proven configuration, one 32-bit LPDDR4 chip, is the most expensive at $29.12.
  • Two 4 Gb DDR3L chips give 1 GB for $20.84, but need two-chip routing (fly-by or T topology).
  • DRAM prices are high right now. In 2026 a 4 Gb chip costs about as much as the RV1106G2 itself.

What switching would mean for the work done

  • The Power sheet (TLV62569 for 0.9 V, EA3036C) would be replaced by an RK809 + TCS4525 tree with a new sequence. The 5 V boost, charger, battery, hold-off and radio power stay.
  • The SoC symbol, footprint and SoC sheet (T11a/b and the drawn part of T11d) would be redone, plus a new DRAM sheet with length-matched routing at layout.
  • The pin map, power budget (P-, F- requirements), and the cost and duty-cycle figures would change. The RK3566 also needs a PMIC sleep scheme to reach its 14 mW suspend.

Decision for you

  • A. Keep the RV1106G3 (recommended if 256 MB is the target). The work already done stands.
  • B. Switch to the RV1106G2 to save about $9 per board, if 128 MB is enough. This means a footprint-compatible part swap and a memory check against the Linux build.
  • C. Switch to the RK3566 + 1 GB LPDDR4 if mainline Linux, more RAM and the low suspend power are worth about $22 more per board plus a 6-layer PCB.
